This work takes the form of a typical recycling bin—something you can find almost anywhere on the streets of Korea or inside public buildings. It is such a familiar object that most of us pass by it every day without giving it a second thought.

However, the artist has given this ordinary trash bin a new status: that of a work of art.

By bringing an object that represents modern social systems and the routines of everyday life into the realm of art, the artist poses a sharp question: What criteria do we use to decide what counts as art?

At the same time, the work humorously captures the way the daily act of sorting and disposing of waste has become a kind of collective social ritual for contemporary society.

Take a moment to look inside the artwork. You may find that its interior has already been filled with pieces of actual trash casually thrown in by visitors. If so, the boundary between the artwork and everyday reality becomes even more blurred, inviting us to reconsider the relationship between art, objects, and the habits that shape our daily lives.
